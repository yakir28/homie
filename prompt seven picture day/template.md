# Picture Day

High-pace property-only vertical real-estate template modeled from the supplied reference video.

## Output

- Format: 9:16
- Target duration: 21 seconds
- Reference count: 8 ordered property images
- Generation: 6 independent 4–5 second handles
- Editing: 7–10 clean scene changes; pace comes from fast camera travel inside each shot
- Audio: rhythmic modern instrumental with tightly synchronized impacts

## Creative principle

The generated handles remain photorealistic and spatially restrained, but the camera must feel energetic. High pace means rapid camera acceleration, fast stabilized travel and decisive braking into each feature reveal. It does not mean a dense montage. Create the speed ramps in editing from smooth generated motion. Never ask the video model to fly continuously through unseen rooms.

## High-pace contract

This template must feel fast from the first frame. Camera velocity is mandatory; cut density is not.

- Use 7–10 clean scene changes across the 21-second film.
- Favor continuous 1.8–3.2 second shots with a visible speed curve inside the shot.
- Every main shot follows `brief orientation → rapid stabilized travel → decisive brake → readable feature reveal`.
- Generated motion stays smooth and constant; editorial ramps create the acceleration and braking.
- Use one dominant physical camera move per clip: drone descent, gimbal push, lateral slider, micro-arc, pedestal rise or pullback.
- Exterior travel may be large and fast. Interior motion stays within the verified view and below five-percent displacement, then receives a strong speed ramp in editing.
- Maintain stable verticals, a realistic 20–28mm real-estate lens and believable gimbal inertia. The camera must never feel like a floating 3D render.
- Hold the destination feature for 0.6–0.9 seconds after braking.
- Use cuts only at verified thresholds, foreground occlusions or matched architectural lines.
- The first meaningful property reveal must occur before 0:01.2.
- The viewer must see exterior, living room and kitchen before 0:08.5.

## Photo order

1. Aerial exterior
2. Front facade
3. Entry
4. Living room

## Timeline

### 0:00–0:03.8 — Picture-day hook

- Start on the aerial exterior.
- Begin moving on frame one; no establishing pause.
- Fast drone descent toward the exact facade, using a three-stage editorial ramp: 1.0x for 0.25 seconds, 2.2x travel, then 0.75x braking into the hero frame.
- Add subtle directional motion blur at peak travel, without hiding the house.
- Resolve on the ground-level facade and brake into a 0.7-second hero hold.
- Kinetic handwritten-style text added in editing:
  - `YOUR LISTING'S ON CAMERA`
  - `MAKE IT COUNT`

### 0:03.8–0:07.2 — Threshold burst

- Clean cut from the facade roof edge to the living-room ceiling beam.
- Use one continuous lateral gimbal move across the verified living-room view: accelerate quickly across the open space, then brake smoothly on the fireplace or seating feature.
- Keep the room readable during travel; do not fragment it into detail cuts.

### 0:07.2–0:11.5 — Kitchen detail rhythm

- Use one continuous micro-arc around the island, with a fast central travel section and a controlled brake on the cabinetry or countertop hero angle.
- Preserve the complete kitchen layout throughout the move; no detail montage and no object-level jump cuts.

### 0:11.5–0:14.5 — Lifestyle rooms

- Fast dining-room push that brakes on the table centerpiece, followed by one clean matched-direction cut.
- Bedroom foreground reveal with a rapid lateral slide behind an existing plant or door edge, braking on the bed.
- No generative room-to-room transition.

### 0:14.5–0:17.2 — Day/night energy beat

- Return to the exact facade or backyard composition.
- Create a short editorial day-to-night-to-day loop from the same locked frame.
- Change lighting only; architecture and landscaping remain unchanged.

### 0:17.2–0:21.0 — Closing payoff

- Backyard lateral reveal with fast stabilized travel, followed by a smooth deceleration into the pullback.
- Cut to the aerial and perform a digital pull-up using the original high-resolution frame if a verified closing aerial is unavailable.
- Finish on a 0.8-second stable hold.
- Final text in editing: `READY FOR ITS CLOSE-UP.`

## Generation prompts

### Handle 1 — aerial to facade

Photorealistic real-estate drone shot of the exact supplied property. Begin on the exact high three-quarter aerial and perform one controlled fast drone descent with a restrained forward approach, keeping the home centered and the horizon stable. End before the roof fills the frame. Lock the facade, roofline, driveway, windows, doors, garage, landscaping, neighboring context, materials, colors and daylight. No architecture changes, invented surroundings, people, cars, text, logos, warping, floating 3D motion or fisheye.

### Handle 2 — facade arrival

Photorealistic ground-level real-estate shot of the exact supplied facade. Perform one low stabilized four-percent push along the existing walkway and decelerate into a centered hero hold. Stop well before the front-door threshold. Lock every exterior element and shadow. No door crossing, geometry drift, new plants, people, cars, text or redesign.

### Handle 3 — living room

Photorealistic real-estate shot of the exact supplied living room. Perform one restrained three-percent lateral slider move with realistic parallax and finish on a stable fireplace or seating hold. Lock the ceiling, beams, chandelier, walls, windows, doors, fireplace, furniture, rug, artwork, floor and decor. Keep the exact same room from first frame to last. Animate the camera only. No new openings, moving furniture, duplicate objects, people, text, logos or redesign.

### Handle 4 — kitchen

Photorealistic real-estate shot of the exact supplied kitchen. Perform one smooth eight-degree micro-arc around the fixed island at constant camera height. Lock every cabinet, appliance, pendant, counter, stool, fixture, doorway and floorboard. Keep the exact same room from first frame to last. Animate the camera only. No geometry drift, cabinet changes, object duplication, people, text, logos or redesign.

### Handle 5 — dining and bedroom generated separately

Generate one restrained three-percent push for the exact dining room and one independent three-percent foreground reveal for the exact bedroom. Do not use a start/end pair between different rooms. Join them with a clean cut on matched camera direction in editing.

### Handle 6 — backyard finale

Photorealistic closing shot of the exact supplied backyard. Begin behind a real existing foreground leaf, wall edge or column and perform one restrained lateral reveal followed by a gentle three-percent pullback. Lock the rear facade, patio, furniture, lawn, fence, trees, shrubs and lighting. Animate only the camera and subtle existing foliage. No landscaping changes, invented structures, people, text, logos or redesign.

## Continuity lock

Treat every supplied image as immutable visual ground truth. Lock architecture, openings, room geometry, furniture identity and count, decor positions, appliances, flooring, landscaping, neighboring context, materials, colors and lighting logic. Keep each source scene unchanged within its generated handle. All room changes are editorial cuts, never generative morphs.

## Editing recipe

- Use constant-speed generated motion, then build speed ramps in the editor.
- Typical exterior ramp: 1.0x orientation → 2.5–3.0x travel → 0.65–0.8x brake → 0.7-second hold.
- Typical interior ramp: 1.0x orientation → 1.8–2.2x travel → 0.7–0.85x brake → 0.6–0.9-second hold.
- Use directional motion blur only during the fastest portion of travel; keep destination frames sharp.
- Use 7–10 clean scene changes in the 21-second master.
- Let each room play as one energetic camera move rather than multiple crops or jump cuts.
- Preserve consistent camera direction across adjacent spaces so momentum continues through the edit.
- Prefer hard cuts, architectural-line matches, real occluder wipes and two-frame exposure flashes.
- Avoid long dissolves, portal transitions, fake doorway travel and AI-generated typography.

## Quality gate

Reject any handle where a window or doorway moves, furniture count changes, cabinetry morphs, the camera crosses solid geometry, the room identity changes, landscaping is invented or motion resembles a floating 3D render.
