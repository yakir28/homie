## Seedance prompt

Create one 30-second, fully silent, portrait 3:4 architectural transformation film as one continuous camera performance. Use every supplied image as a fixed chronological visual anchor in the exact order provided. They depict one ordinary, attainable suburban home and its matching drawing states. Never reorder, merge, redesign, upscale, beautify, or replace the house or rooms. The drawing and the real property are the same geometry: roofline, garage, porch, columns, doors, windows, room footprint, furniture and materials must remain aligned with their reference images.

The visual idea is a living blueprint that becomes a real home. All transitions happen as visible events attached to the property's own plotted geometry. Do not use cuts, fades, dissolves, editorial wipes, split screens, screen-space boundaries, or a moving line that divides drawing from reality. Once an element becomes real, it remains real. Use deep blueprint paper #16233F, crisp draft-white CAD linework #EAF2FF, and one cold green-biased acid-chartreuse accent #D1EF17. CAD lines are honest one-pixel vector lines with a faint millimeter grid, hatching, ticks and arrowheads, with zero glow or bloom. Reality is authentic MLS photography with ordinary material wear, stable verticals and believable light, never glossy CGI.

0.0–3.5 seconds — Begin face-on over the supplied empty blueprint grid. A single cold chartreuse centerline plots through frame center. Draft-white strokes grow from it at pen speed and resolve exactly into the supplied matching front elevation. The camera begins almost static, then starts a very shallow clockwise arc. No readable text appears.

3.5–7.0 seconds — The completed drawing plane tilts backward into depth without any line disappearing. While the camera continues the same shallow arc, the elevation resolves into the supplied three-quarter axonometric wireframe of the exact house. Garage, porch, columns, windows, roof planes and driveway assemble from their own lines. Keep the house centered in the middle 70 percent of the portrait frame.

7.0–12.0 seconds — The chartreuse route line reaches the driveway and front door, then behaves like current running through the plotted geometry. Matter grows out of the connected drawing strokes: concrete driveway and steps gain mass, brick and beige stucco set in place, the garage and green front door become real, glass appears inside its exact mullions, roof shingles form on their own plotted planes, and ordinary lawn and shrubs develop around the footprint. Materialization travels cleanly from ground level upward and follows the supplied transformation reference. Blueprint paper develops into the supplied cloudy suburban daylight from the property silhouette outward. Never create a straight wipe edge. Ease the camera from axonometric orbit toward the real exterior camera height.

12.0–15.0 seconds — The now-real exterior matches the supplied exterior identity anchor exactly. Hold a readable natural view while the camera rises slightly and tilts toward a plumb top-down angle. At the top of the move, the ground plane becomes the supplied matching blueprint floor plan; this is a perspective flattening of the same geometry, not a cut.

15.0–19.0 seconds — Descend vertically into the floor plan. Plan lines extrude upward past the lens into the supplied real living room: walls gain height, warm-oak laminate rolls into place, the gray sofa and olive chair settle from their exact plan symbols, and daylight appears through the correct front window. Finish at normal eye height with a restrained three-percent forward glide. Lock all furniture, walls, openings and object counts.

19.0–22.5 seconds — Continue the same forward direction. Let a real doorway edge fill the frame for a very brief natural occlusion, then reveal the supplied kitchen and dining reference. Do not invent a corridor or morph the rooms together. Execute a gentle two-percent lateral slide; lock cabinets, appliances, table, chairs, windows, ceiling and floor.

22.5–25.5 seconds — Move toward the supplied rear opening and use the dark door frame as another momentary in-world occlusion. Reveal the supplied backyard and exact rear facade. Glide backward no more than three percent while only existing leaves and grass move in a light breeze. Do not add a pool, deck, structure, furniture, planting or neighbor.

25.5–29.0 seconds — The backward move rises and carries us continuously around to the supplied front-exterior composition. Draft-white proof lines plot directly on top of the real roof edges, garage, porch columns, door and windows, landing exactly on their material twins. A few cold chartreuse dimension arrows snap between real architectural levels. The lines then sink into the surfaces without displacing them as daylight warms slightly toward a natural late golden hour.

29.0–30.0 seconds — Settle on the real front facade centered in frame and hold for one full second. No generated wording. Reserve clean negative space for the Homie editor to add the listing title and CTA after generation.

Camera and optics: one virtual 28 mm full-frame architectural lens for the entire film, stable verticals, subtle natural barrel character, realistic motion blur only during the descent, no handheld shake, no speed ramps, no crash zoom, no FPV movement, no lens flare, no light streaks and no floating bokeh. Every movement blends with a smooth S-curve velocity and the camera never fully stops before the final hold.

Hard exclusions: no people, pets, cars, signs, house numbers, readable text, stray letters, subtitles, logos or watermarks. No luxury redesign, new rooms, new doors, changed windows, moving furniture, altered object count, geometry drift, ghosting, flicker, frame blending, broken glass, impossible camera passage, straight transformation frontier, or half-blueprint/half-reality split screen.
